# Deep Research

Perform systematic research rather than answering from memory.

## When to use

Use for:

- Complex research questions
- Technology evaluations
- Product comparisons
- Architecture decisions
- Market research
- Literature reviews
- Policy/regulation research
- Questions involving multiple competing claims
- Requests for comprehensive analysis

Do not use unnecessarily for simple factual questions.

## Research process

### Phase 1 — Define

Determine:

- What exactly is being asked?
- What decisions need to be made?
- What facts are required?
- What constraints matter?
- What information could have changed recently?

Break the problem into 3–8 research questions.

### Phase 2 — Search

Use Brave Search.

For each research question:

1. Search broadly.
2. Identify authoritative sources.
3. Search specifically for conflicting information.
4. Search for recent developments.

### Phase 3 — Verify

Prefer:

1. Primary sources
2. Official documentation
3. Government sources
4. Academic sources
5. Reputable technical/industry sources
6. Community reports

Do not treat multiple websites repeating the same claim as independent confirmation.

### Phase 4 — Analyze

Separate:

- Established facts
- Strong evidence
- Reasonable inference
- Uncertainty
- Conflicting evidence

Do not manufacture consensus.

### Phase 5 — Synthesize

Produce:

1. Executive summary
2. Key findings
3. Evidence
4. Important disagreements
5. Practical implications
6. Recommendation, if appropriate

### Source discipline

For important claims:

- Prefer recent sources.
- Prefer primary sources.
- Verify surprising claims.
- Identify the source behind important statistics.
- Avoid citing search snippets as if they were full evidence.

## Final answer

Be concise relative to the amount of research performed.

Do not dump every search result on the user.

Present the conclusions and the evidence needed to understand them.
